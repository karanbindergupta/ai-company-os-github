# Why diaspora travellers use agents — first pass

## Summary
The founder's premise assumes a **UX problem**: booking is hard, so people use agents. The
evidence so far points to a **structural inventory problem** instead. Agents can see and sell
fares that consumers are structurally prevented from seeing online. If that holds, a better
booking interface does not compete with an agent — it is strictly worse, because it cannot show
the cheaper fare.

## Finding 1 — consolidator net fares are hidden from consumers by design
> "Net fares are restricted from online display, so travel agents access them through a Global
> Distribution System (GDS), New Distribution Capability (NDC), or the WINGS Booking Platform."
> — Sky Bird Travel [S1], Tier 2 (consolidator's own site), retrieved 2026-09-07

**Type: FACT** (vendor describing its own mechanism). **Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH.**

## Finding 2 — the discount is material
Consolidators buy in bulk and resell to agents at net fares claimed at **45–60% off published
fares** [S2, travelprofessionalnews.com, Tier 2/3].

**Type: INFERENCE / unverified claim.** **Confidence: LOW.** This is an industry-publication
figure, not a primary source, and the publication serves travel agents — it has an interest in
the number looking large. **Must be verified against an actual consolidator contract or a
diaspora agent's real quotes before any pricing model uses it.**

## Finding 3 — the direct question returned weak evidence
A search for why South Asian diaspora specifically use agents returned generic "benefits of a
travel agent" content and forum posts. **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE** on the community-specific
motivations (trust, language, credit terms, visa assistance, cash payment, family multi-city
routing). These are plausible and commonly cited, but I have not evidenced them and will not
state them as findings.

## What this changes
If Finding 1 is correct, the roadmap question is not "how do we make booking easier" but
**"how do we get access to the inventory agents have"** — as a licensed consolidator, an IATA
accredited agent, through NDC direct-airline deals, or by aggregating existing agents.

## What must be established next (blocking)
1. Verify net-fare access requirements — IATA accreditation, consolidator agreements, bonding.
2. Verify the real discount on the routes that matter (LHR/Europe → DEL, KHI, DAC, LOS, ACC).
3. Establish the actual reasons diaspora customers stay with agents — primary research, not inference.
4. Establish whether NDC has changed airline willingness to sell direct at those fares.
