# FOUNDER DECISION PACKAGE — reframe the premise
**From:** Nadia Okonkwo, CEO · **Evidence:** Kwame Boateng, Amara Diallo · **Risk:** RISK-001

## Decision required
Does the company pursue "easier booking for diaspora travellers", or reframe around **inventory
access** — the thing that appears to actually keep customers with agents?

## Recommendation
**Reframe.** Treat inventory access as the core problem and easy booking as table stakes.

## Why
Consolidator net fares are *restricted from online display* by design [S1]. If a customer's agent
can quote a fare our platform structurally cannot show, no amount of UX wins that customer. We
would be competing on convenience against someone competing on price — and losing.

## Evidence and its limits
- **MEDIUM-HIGH:** net fares exist and are withheld from consumer display [S1, Tier 2].
- **LOW:** the 45–60% discount figure [S2] is from a publication serving agents. Unverified.
- **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE:** the community-specific reasons — trust, language, credit, visa help,
  cash payment, complex family routing. Plausible, widely assumed, **not evidenced by us**.

## Alternatives
1. **Consumer OTA with better UX** — rejected: cannot access the inventory that matters.
2. **Become the consolidator-backed seller** — get IATA accreditation / consolidator agreements
   and sell net fares direct. Highest upside, highest regulatory and capital cost.
3. **Agent-enablement platform** — sell software to the thousands of small diaspora agencies
   instead of competing with them. Lower capital, slower ceiling, and they already have the
   customer relationship.
4. **Hybrid** — start as (3) to learn the real economics, move to (2) once volume justifies it.

## Tradeoffs
Option 2 is a regulated financial-adjacent business, not a website. Option 3 sells to a
fragmented, price-sensitive, low-trust buyer. Both are harder than the original idea — and both
are actually defensible, which the original idea was not.

## Risks
- RISK-001 (high): premise may be wrong; a UI-first build would be wasted.
- Regulatory exposure: selling flights in the EU/UK involves accreditation and consumer-protection
  obligations. Alistair Fenn will scope this; **we will recommend qualified counsel, not give legal advice**.

## What I need from you
1. **Approve the reframe** (or tell me to keep the original premise and I will test it harder).
2. Confirm appetite for a **regulated** business — this changes what we are building.
3. One fact only you have: do you personally know diaspora travel agents or customers we could
   talk to? Primary access would collapse the biggest evidence gap in days rather than weeks.
