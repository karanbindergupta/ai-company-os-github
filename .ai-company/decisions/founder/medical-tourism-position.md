# FOUNDER DECISION PACKAGE — which position in medical tourism
**From:** Nadia Okonkwo, CEO · **Evidence:** Oren Ashkenazi, Amara Diallo · **Risks:** RISK-003, RISK-004

## Decision required
Medical tourism is attractive, but the standard entry is structurally compromised. Which position
do we pursue?

## Recommendation
**Option B — supply-side orchestration — as the first move.** Not because it is the biggest idea,
but because it is the one a solo pre-revenue founder can actually validate, and it does not
require us to solve the trust gap before earning anything.

## The four options

**A. Facilitator / marketplace** — the obvious one. **Recommend against.**
Paid by the clinic per booking. Unlicensed in most jurisdictions. Structurally misaligned with the
patient [S8]. We would be entering as part of the problem, competing with incumbents who are
better at the thing we would be doing badly.

**B. Supply-side orchestration** — sell the missing journey layer to mid-tier hospitals.
Named buyer, budget, and a measurable pain: they already have the demand and lose it at 12
fragmented handoffs [S9]. B2B. No patient-safety liability, no facilitator regulation, no trust-gap
exposure. Ceiling is lower and it is unglamorous. **This is the tractable one.**

**C. Buy-side patient agent** — paid by the patient, not the clinic. Independent clinical review of
treatment plans before travel. [S8] names independent specialist review as *"the highest-leverage
protective step."* Genuinely aligned, genuinely defensible — and it rests on one unproven
assumption: **that patients will pay directly for advice.** That is currently INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.

**D. Outcome infrastructure** — the cross-border outcome registry [S8] says is one of the few things
that could actually close the gap. Highest ceiling, hardest to start, needs institutional
participation we cannot compel. A ten-year business, not a first one.

## Tradeoffs
B earns sooner and teaches us the industry from the inside — including which clinics are actually
good, which is the asset C and D both need. C is the more defensible business but starts with an
unvalidated payer. D is a real prize with no viable path for a solo founder today.

**B is also a legitimate route into C**: run orchestration for mid-tier hospitals, accumulate real
outcome and conversion data, then use that as the credibility base for a buy-side product.

## Risks
- **RISK-003 (high):** the marketplace shape is misaligned — this recommendation avoids it.
- **RISK-004 (high):** facilitator regulation and delayed-failure liability are **unresearched**.
  Alistair scopes this before any model is committed. **We will recommend qualified counsel, not
  give legal advice.**
- Option B's buyer — mid-tier hospitals — has **unproven willingness to buy software**.

## What is NOT established
Market size (the $100B figure is vendor marketing, unverified). Whether patients pay for advice.
Whether mid-tier hospitals buy. The size of the mid-tier conversion gap — HFS states plainly it is
not publicly verifiable.
