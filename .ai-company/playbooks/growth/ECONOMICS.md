# GROWTH ECONOMICS
**Growth cannot optimize acquisition independently of economics.** A channel that converts at a
CAC the business cannot afford is not a growth channel — it is a way to lose money faster.

## Required for every significant acquisition initiative
| Metric | Definition | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| **CAC** | Fully loaded cost per *acquired* customer | Using cost per signup, not per paying customer |
| **LTV** | Contribution margin × expected lifetime | Using revenue instead of margin |
| **Payback** | Months to recover CAC | Ignoring it — a 24-month payback is a cash-flow problem |
| Conversion | Per funnel step | Reporting only the aggregate |
| Retention | Cohort curve | Averaging cohorts of different ages |
| Margin | Contribution margin per customer | Forgetting COGS |

## Rules
1. **Never invent a benchmark.** Source it or model an explicit range (`playbooks/research.md`).
2. **LTV uses contribution margin**, not revenue. Revenue-based LTV overstates every ratio.
3. **Payback period matters as much as the LTV/CAC ratio** — a healthy ratio with 30-month payback
   can still kill a pre-revenue company.
4. Model base / upside / **downside**. If it only closes in the upside case, it does not close.
5. **The CFO reviews every channel model.** Growth proposes; finance validates.

## Saying no
When the economics do not work, say so plainly and early. That is the highest-value thing this
department does — and the Acquisition Specialist's role pack requires it:
> "Say plainly when a channel cannot work economically."

**All paid spend is founder-approved.** Growth models and recommends; it never commits.
