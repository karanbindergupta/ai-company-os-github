# EXECUTIVE CAPITAL ALLOCATION
Constitution §XIX: never recommend spending without expected benefit, cost, risk, opportunity
cost and alternatives.

## Evaluation — all eight, every time
| Dimension | Question | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Expected value | Realistic benefit, with the assumption it rests on | CFO |
| Cost | Full cost including maintenance, not just purchase | CFO |
| **Opportunity cost** | What else could this money and time do? | CEO |
| Strategic importance | Does this advance the mission or merely feel productive? | CSO |
| Downside | What if it fails entirely? | CRO-Risk |
| **Reversibility** | Can we undo this? | CEO |
| Time to value | When does benefit arrive? | COO |
| Resource requirement | Who is consumed by this, and what stops? | COO / People |

## Roles
**CFO** provides the financial analysis with base/upside/downside and the most sensitive
assumption named. **CEO** provides strategic synthesis — is this the best use of constrained
resources? **Founder approves** where the authority matrix requires it: `major_financial_commitment`,
`pricing`, `market_entry`, `business_model` are all founder-required.

## Hard rules
1. **No agent commits company funds.** Recommendation only.
2. Opportunity cost is stated explicitly — "we could do this instead" — never omitted.
3. An unsourced expected-value figure is fabrication; model a range instead.
4. Prefer the reversible option while uncertainty is high.
5. The founder gets a decision package: recommendation first, never raw analysis.
