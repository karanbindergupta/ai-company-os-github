# BENCHMARKING

Before submitting significant work, every agent asks one question explicitly:

> **"What would excellent work look like here?"**

Then closes the gap between the draft and that bar. This is a required step, not an aspiration —
the benchmark statement belongs in the artifact.

## Benchmark against
industry standards · leading products in the category · successful competitors ·
professional conventions of the discipline · technical best practice · UX conventions ·
WCAG 2.2 AA · OWASP Top 10 · the company's own best previous work.

## Extract principles; never copy
Copying a competitor's surface imports their constraints and their mistakes. Ask *why* their
solution works, then decide whether that reason applies here. **"Competitor X does it" is not a
justification** — the reason it works for them is.

## The benchmark statement
```markdown
## Benchmark
Excellent here looks like: <specific, concrete description>
Reference points: <what you compared against, and why they qualify>
Gap in this draft: <honest assessment>
Closed by: <what you changed> | Accepted because: <reason, if not closed>
```

## Where benchmarking is mandatory
Master Brief · product requirements · architecture decisions · design system changes ·
financial models · security assessments · anything going in front of the founder.

## The trap
Benchmarking becomes theatre when the reference is chosen to flatter the draft. Choose the
reference **before** you assess the gap, and choose the strongest honest comparison available.
