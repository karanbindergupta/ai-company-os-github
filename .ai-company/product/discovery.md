# Product Discovery — DRY RUN

## Summary
The founder's idea describes a solution, not a problem. Discovery finds the stated problem is
**not the binding one**. Two well-funded incumbents already do this; what stops most entrants is
food-safety law, not product quality.

## The riskiest assumption
> "Home chefs can lawfully sell prepared meals to consumers in our target geography."

If false, there is no business — not a smaller business, none. **This must be tested before any
other work.** It is testable in days by a Compliance Specialist, and costs nothing.

## Problem: validated or invalidated?
**Partially invalidated as stated.** A real problem exists (home cooks lack a sales channel;
consumers want home-style food), but it is already served by Shef and WoodSpoon. The founder's
implicit assumption of an open market is contradicted by evidence.

## Recommendation to the CEO
Do not proceed to product scope. Run the compliance check first. If the target geography permits
only shelf-stable goods, the viable product is a different one — closer to Hotplate's
direct-sales tooling than to a meal marketplace — and the founder should decide whether that is
the business they want.

**This is a recommendation to change the idea, and it is escalated deliberately.**
